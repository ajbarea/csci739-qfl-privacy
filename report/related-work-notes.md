## 1. Kumar, Heredge, Li, Eloul, Sureshbabu, Pistoia (JPMorgan), arXiv:2309.13002v2
"Expressive variational quantum circuits provide inherent privacy in federated learning"

### Claim and qualifiers
- Abstract: "expressive maps lead to inherent privacy against gradient inversion attacks, while overparameterization ensures model trainability." Privacy "centers on the complexity of solving the system of high-degree multivariate Chebyshev polynomials generated by the gradients." Also: "establish a direct connection between overparameterization in the original federated learning model and underparameterization in the attack model."
- Evidence is (a) complexity ARGUMENTS (Sec VI C.2-C.3: Buchberger worst-case bound, Nyquist sampling count), (b) numerics for ONE attack family: ADAM on the l2 gradient-matching loss (Sec VII). No proof of hardness; "compelling arguments", "numerical scaling arguments" (abstract). Local-minima count bound is Bezout upper bound (Eq 93), "for a general system of polynomials"; they say in practice minima track the highest frequency.
- Qualifiers that matter:
  - Privacy is relative to an attacker that is the honest-but-curious server running gradient-descent (ADAM) from random inits on the loss L (Eq 88/100). Fig 4/5: success is "no better than guessing at random" for m=3 (R^2=0.993 linear P(eps) vs eps). They concede "there may be other stochastic optimization methods that have a higher probability of reaching a solution eventually" (Sec VII A).
  - Hardness is stated in m = qubits PER INPUT DIMENSION (exponential in m); at m=1,2 distribution of minima is "slightly elevated... close to the global minimum" (Sec VII B), Fig 5 right: "lower m values exhibit worse privacy". Only "by the time we reach m = 3 and onwards" is it uniform.
  - Worst-case-for-client setting: batch size B=1, noiseless circuits, exact gradients (Sec VI intro: "worst-case data-leakage scenario"). Larger B and noise are asserted to help the client, not tested.
  - Label y is assumed known: for y in {-1,+1} sign of gradient reveals it (Eq 75 and text); "we assume the worst case label leakage scenario where the server can ascertain the value of the labels perfectly". Attack target is only x.
  - Privacy definition (Eq 74/76): server outputs x' with ||x'-x|| <= eps (eps>0 arbitrary).
  - Requires gradients to be non-low-frequency: Sec VII D, Fig 12: if the FL model is trained to fit y=0.7cos x with m=2, it "suppresses high-frequency terms. This leads to a simplified gradient attack model loss landscape with reduced privacy." With m=4 on the same target the high-frequency terms persist (Fig 13), privacy maintained. So privacy depends on m being large relative to target complexity and on trained theta.
  - Theory also notes the feature-space route (Sec VI C.7): gradients are quadratic in rho(x); "easier than solving for x directly", but writing equations needs exponential simulation and inverting rho(x)->x still hits periodic local minima (Eq 97-98).
  - "no practical purely classical machine learning attack" (supervised gradient->x model) argued via Nyquist sample scaling (end of Sec VI C.4), not tested.

### Threat model (Sec VI A "Information available to the server")
Honest-but-curious server knows: all d gradient values; batch size B; encoding map architecture U (not x); ansatz architecture W AND parameters theta (server sends them, "can completely replicate"); measurement operator O; the cost function (MSE). Sees labels indirectly via gradient sign (above). Unknowns: B(n+|C|) per client, d equations (Sec VI B).

### Circuit / model
- Encoding ("Fourier tower map", Eq 14-15, Fig 3): U(x)=tensor_{j=1..n} tensor_{k=1..m} R_X(5^{k-1} gamma x_j), gamma=2pi; Nq = n*m qubits. (Ref [29] original uses beta_jk=3^{k-1} with Z; this paper uses 5^{k-1} to get dense gradient spectrum.) Parallel (product) encoding, ONE upload per feature (no sequential re-uploading); the "expressivity" is the exponentially spaced frequencies. Model-output spectrum: Eq 15 text; gradient/cost spectrum size |Omega_g| = 5^m per dim, max frequency d_F=(5^m-1)/2 (Eq 68-72); m = log5(2 d_F+1) (Eq 70). Multivariate: 2n unknowns [c_k,s_k], degree (d_F)^n (below Eq 86).
- Ansatz: hardware efficient ansatz (HEA), L blocks of [R_Y R_Z per qubit + nearest-neighbour CNOT]; d = 2 L Nq (Eq 17). 
- Overparameterization (Sec VI A, Eq 73): "Num. param >= 4 Nq ... a sufficient condition for overparameterisation of the hardware efficient ansatz [33]". Intro/Sec VI A text says the parameter count "scales exponentially with the number of qubits"; Eq 73 as printed is only linear in Nq -- internally inconsistent (flag; Heredge 2405.08801 reads it as exponential depth). The notion is from ref [33] (Larocca et al. overparameterization), NOT defined via DLA dimension in this paper; DLA is never mentioned.
- Observable: tensor of Pauli Z over all qubits (Fig 3 caption: "Pauli operator ⊗Z"); output y in [-1,1]. Cost: MSE (Eq 21). Gradients via parameter-shift (Eq 26-29).
- Numerics: univariate n=1 (m=1..6 for minima counts; m=3 for ADAM attack; m=4 and m=2 for training-time landscapes), n=2 two-dim landscapes for m=1,2 (Fig 8). Number of HEA layers L / parameter count d actually simulated: NOT STATED in text -> UNVERIFIED. Simulation: Qiskit, full landscape grid.

### Attack method (numerics)
- Loss: l2 gradient match, L = sum_j (dCost'/dtheta_j - dCost/dtheta_j)^2 (Eq 88); in landscape analysis a single random gradient j (excluding final layer) is used, L_j (Eq 100).
- Optimizer: ADAM, lr 0.01, 60 iterations; gradient of L w.r.t. x' by nested parameter-shift (Eq 90-91) -> exact (analytic), not finite differences. Restarts: 100 experiments x 10 random x' inits each (Fig 4 caption); theta fixed random; x random.
- Success criterion: |x-x'| < eps with circular distance (Eq 99), varying eps (Fig 5). No single fixed threshold. Fig 4 shows histogram of error ~uniform; also shows gradient-loss can be small while |x'-x| large (Sec VI C.5 "decorrelation", Fig 9).
- No Kalman filtering / low-pass filtering / alternative optimizers.

### Quantitative results
- Number of local minima, univariate: N_c = 0.622 e^{1.484 m} (Eq 101, Fig 6; 10 repeated runs, random theta). n=2 also exponential.
- Spacing global-min to nearest local max: r = 3.34 e^{-1.49 m} (Eq 102, Fig 10).
- Local minima density near the global minimum: elevated for m=1,2, uniform from m>=3 (Fig 9).
- ADAM at m=3 (n=1): error uniform on [0,pi]; P(success)=linear in eps, R^2=0.993 (Fig 4-5). No success-rate-at-fixed-eps table.
- Analytical bounds: Buchberger worst case O(sqrt(5^{m n} 4^n))-style scaling (their Eq 87 -> text "O(sqrt(5^{mn} 4^n))" extraction garbled; UNVERIFIED exact form); Nyquist: >= (5^m-1)^n gradient samples; Bezout bound on minima 4^{n^2}((5^m-1)/2)^{n}... (Eq 93; garbled in PDF text, UNVERIFIED exact form).

### Noise
Not studied. Sec VI: noiseless/exact gradients are "the best case scenario for the server"; "a setting with experimental errors would introduce further noise in the cost function gradients, thus making it harder to learn the data input [64]" (assertion, untested). No shot noise analysis. (Contrast: Papadopoulos below suggests added noise helps privacy; Kumar does not test.)

### Stated limitations / future work (Sec VIII)
- Open: whether classical ML can navigate high-frequency periodic feature spaces; "efficacy of quantum encodings which are hard to simulate classically"; whether classical FL could use periodic feature maps. No limitations section per se. Batch size B>1, noise, other attackers untested.

### "Overparameterized" definitions used
(i) FL model: HEA with d >= 4Nq (Eq 73, citing [33]) so no spurious local minima, trainable; (ii) attack model is "underparameterized": n unknowns x' vs Nq=nm qubits / high-frequency expressive map (Sec VII B). No Lie-algebra definition.

## 2. Papadopoulos, Eloul, Satsangi, Heredge, Kumar, Chen, Pistoia, arXiv:2504.12806v2
"A Numerical Gradient Inversion Attack in Variational Quantum Neural-Networks" (cs.LG; JPMorgan)

### Claim and qualifiers
- Abstract: scheme "successfully reconstructs input training, real-world, practical data from trainable VQNNs' gradients"; "can invert even batch-trained data, given the VQNN model is sufficiently 'overparameterized'."
- Conclusion: "as long as the model is trainable (numerically), the proposed attack can invert successfully gradients to data within the precision of the numerical scheme, error down to ~1e-10." (But see success rates below: single-shot success 40-100%, not 100%.)
- Direct rebuttal of Kumar et al. Sec I: refs [27] (=Kumar 2309.13002) etc. "highlight VQCs as resistant"; Sec II: "Here, we show empirically, that despite this challenge, we can successfully invert to original data in trainable VQNN models." NOTE: same author group (Eloul, Heredge, Kumar, Pistoia overlap). They do not evaluate Kumar's exact Fourier-tower m>=3 construction; models are different (below). Also cites a Lie-algebra relation [61]=Heredge (2405.08801).
- Qualifiers:
  - Models are SMALL: 2 and 6 qubits/inputs, angle encoding RX, one qubit per feature. No exponential-frequency encoding.
  - 6-qubit attacks succeed only 40-70% of single trials (Table II); 2-qubit 100%.
  - Attack run on UNTRAINED models (theta = all-ones vector) mainly; random weights Fig 16 and a trained model Fig 17 (Fraud ZZEfficient 6 qubits, 3 attacks, "converge to as low as 1e-5") as supplementary.
  - Batch: only B=2 and B=3 attacked, with a "Simple (Entangled)" 2-qubit model (Table II); text: "it is enough to use a batch of size above 3 to not be able to recover the gradients in the VQNN model used." Batch >3 not attacked; they argue large batch is no defence since test performance degrades (Cosine acc 93% at B=5, 88% B=20, 71% B=60, 66% B=100, Sec VI B).
  - Label handling UNCLEAR: Sec V E.1 says "there is no access to the original underlying data x neither to the target values y"; Sec II says attack works "without relying on target labels". But MSE gradients depend on y and the text never says how y enters the proxy gradient g' -> UNVERIFIED how labels are handled (contrast Kumar: label assumed recoverable).

### Threat model
Server/adversary sees shared gradient g (sum over batch for B>1); knows model f, theta (initial weights set to ones, or random), feature map, ansatz, observable; initializes x' at random; computes proxy gradient g'=parameter-shift on x' (Eq 6). Setting is FL with client gradients Gi (Eq 1). No x, "no y" (see caveat).

### Circuit / model (Table I, Fig 6)
- Encoding: angle encoding R_X(x_j), one qubit per input dimension (J=q); entangling feature map variant ZZFeatureMap (Qiskit) used with EfficientSU2 ansatz as "ZZEfficient" for Fraud (Fig 6d). Single upload; no re-uploading.
- Ansatz: R_Y/R_Z rotations + CNOT ("Simple", "Complex", "Simple (Entangled)" Fig 6a-c; EfficientSU2 for ZZEfficient). Qubits/inputs: 2 and 6. Parameter counts: only Fig 8 is stated -- 2-qubit ZZEfficient ansatz with 8, 12, 16, 20 parameters; counts for other models are in Fig 6 diagrams -> UNVERIFIED. Observable: Pauli-Z typical (Sec III-A "common choice"); exact per-model observable UNVERIFIED. Loss MSE. Libraries Qiskit + PennyLane + Torch.
- Datasets: cos (y=0.7 cos x, regression, R^2 train 98-99%/test 94-98%), MNIST (2 or 6 dims binary, balanced acc test ~80-81%), Credit Card Fraud (balanced acc test 78-86%). 5-fold CV.
- Definition of "over-parameterized" (Sec V E.4): "The inversion is possible as long as the model (the number of equations can be constructed from theta) is comparable or larger than the number of unknowns (x, y) [85]." Pure counting: equations (#params M) vs unknowns (B(J + labels)). NOT Lie-algebra dimension. They note VQNNs are small so batch>1 makes them "under-parameterized". Sec V C: the entangled model "ensures an 'Over-parameterized' model" (assertion).

### Attack method (Sec IV, Alg 2)
- Loss L_{g',g} = (1/M) sum_m (g'_m - g_m)^2 (Eq 7).
- Gradient of L w.r.t. x': central FINITE DIFFERENCES (Eq 8) with step h, averaged over n=1..N symmetric offsets (n+1)h as a moving-average LOW-PASS filter (Eq 9); N initially large, halved (N <- max(1, N//2)) when mean-loss ratio L^k/L^{k-1} > threshold (adaptive window). Each loss evaluation needs parameter-shift gradients of the circuit at x'+-(n+1)h e_j (Alg 2 lines 6-14), i.e. many circuit evaluations.
- Update: plain SGD on x' (Eq 10), lr; optional Kalman filter on the iterates (Eq 11-13, App A; F,H,Q,P,R init all-ones, zero state; "we do not assume a prior"). Kalman variant improves convergence (Fig 9: with Kalman N=16 succeeds; non-Kalman N=16 fails, N=64 succeeds).
- Restarts/trials: 10 attacks from random x' per model; "single-shot" = success within <=200-250 iterations (Table II caption "Maximum iterations <= 250"; text "up to 200"). Specific h, lr, N0, threshold values: not given in text -> UNVERIFIED.
- Success criterion: MSE(x',x) <= 0.005 (Sec V E.3). Note they call it "Euclidean distance" via "Eq 7" (mislabelled; Eq 7 is the gradient loss). Longer runs reach ~1e-10 (App Fig 15, only for initial x' >= 0.005 away "constrained to >= 0.005 random distances" -- i.e. starts not arbitrarily near x).

### Quantitative results (Table II, mean MSE of 10 attacks, +-SE)
| model | one-shot success, adaptive MA | MSE | one-shot success, Kalman | MSE |
| 2q Cosine Complex | 100% | 0.0002 (7e-4) | 100% | 0.0002 (5e-4) |
| 6q Cosine Complex | 40% | 0.0005 (4e-4) | 70% | 0.0003 (3e-4) |
| 6-dim Fraud ZZEfficient | 50% | 0.0007 (2e-4) | 70% | 0.0003 (1e-4) |
| 6-dim MNIST Complex | 40% | 0.0005 (1e-4) | 80% | 0.0002 (1e-4) |
| Batch 2 | 100% | 5e-5 (1e-5) | 100% | 2e-5 (4e-5) |

(Table II row labels for batch rows: "VQNN Batch size 2/3"; Simple Entangled 2-qubit per Sec V C.) Fig 8: 2-qubit ZZEfficient with 8/12/16/20 parameters: all recovered to 1e-5, iterations grow with depth. Table III (single attack each): NN Fraud 16 weights DLG mse <0.001 in 10 iters; Kalman on VQNN <=0.017 (noise 0.08 cos) in 200 iters; DLG "fails for VQNN" (text Sec VI C).
- Scaling gap relevant to Kumar: 6-qubit success is 40-80% (not 100%) and needs 200+ iterations of expensive FD; no sweep over qubit count beyond 2 vs 6, so no scaling curve vs m.

### Noise
- Gaussian noise (std sigma) added to shared gradients (a DP-style defence), NOT shot/hardware noise in the attack. Cos 2-qubit VQNN: sigma 0.0-0.08 (Fig 10); Fraud 6-dim: sigma 0.0, 0.05, 0.10, 0.14 (Fig 11). "VQNNs offer better security against inversion with noise, while NNs remain vulnerable even with degraded performance" for Cosine; at sigma=0.1 Fraud VQNN inversion error ~0.013 MSE with R^2=0.65 (text; "R^2" for a classification task as printed, sic). Table III: Fraud NN +noise0.14: DLG <0.06; VQNN cos +noise0.08 Kalman <0.007.
- Remark (Sec VI C end): hardware noise "often reduced in VQNN gradients [86], but can be leveraged here for privacy... advanced low-error quantum computers may not be essential." Not experimentally tested. No shot-noise simulation of the attack (UNVERIFIED absence beyond text search: grep for "shot" finds only "single-shot"/"one-shot" trial wording).

### Stated limitations / future work
- "does not intend to provide a full assessment to all potential models or datasets"; "forms the base for a benchmark scheme."
- Batch>3 not recoverable on their small models (but they argue bigger batches hurt accuracy); "as long as the model is trainable"; future: use low-pass filtering to improve VQNN training. No explicit limitation on shot noise.

## 3. Heredge, Kumar, Herman, Chakrabarti, Yalovetzky, Sureshbabu, Li, Pistoia
Published: "Characterizing privacy in quantum machine learning", npj Quantum Information 11, 80 (published 19 May 2025), DOI 10.1038/s41534-025-01022-z (verified via Crossref API; Crossref lists vol 11, article-number 80).
arXiv: **2405.08801** (v1 14 May 2024, v2 15 May 2024), arXiv title "Prospects of Privacy Advantage in Quantum Machine Learning". (A 2026 Springer chapter "Characterizing Privacy of Quantum Variational Circuit Models", DOI 10.1007/978-3-032-11153-1_15, also exists.)
CAVEAT: all section/eq numbers below are from arXiv v2 (May 2024). The npj text (retitled, 2025) was NOT read (paywall/redirect) -> any npj changes UNVERIFIED. Cite the npj version only for claims confirmed there.

### Claim and qualifiers
- Abstract: "properties conducive to the trainability of VQCs, such as a polynomial-sized DLA, also facilitate the extraction of detailed snapshots of the input. We term this a weak privacy breach, as the snapshots enable training VQC models for distinct learning tasks without direct access to the original input." Strong breach (recover x) "by classical or quantum-assisted polynomial time methods" requires conditions on the encoding: "classical simulatability, overlap with DLA basis, and its Fourier frequency characteristics."
- Definitions: snapshot e_snap with [e_snap]_alpha = Tr(B_alpha rho(x)) over an orthonormal DLA basis (Def 6, Eq 12): weak breach = snapshot recovery in poly time. Strong breach = Def 7 (classical) or Def 8 (quantum-assisted) snapshot inversion: ||x'-x||_2 <= eps with prob >= 2/3, poly(d,1/eps); up to periodicity (x + k pi).
- Theorem 2 (Snapshot Recovery): needs LASA (iO in DLA g, Def 5), slow Pauli expansion (Def 9), and number of parameters D >= dim(g); then recovery by solving D x dim(g) linear system (Eq 25, Alg 2), O(poly(dim g)) (Gaussian elimination O(dim(g)^3)). Corollary 5 (Sec V A): trainable LASA VQC + slow Pauli expansion + poly DLA => snapshot recoverable in poly(n). Table I summarizes.
- Strong breach results: Thm 3 / Alg 3: Pauli product encoding (Eq 26) AND parallel re-upload R_X(alpha_l x_j) incl. Fourier tower map (Eq 27-28) classically invertible in O(poly(n) log(1/eps)) if poly DLA + slow Pauli expansion and the DLA overlaps span{iZ_j, iY_j} (else FAILURE). Thm 4 / Alg 4: general separable Pauli encodings with dim(x_J)=O(1), each x_k encoded <= R=O(poly(n)) times, |S_J| >= dim(x_J) -> quantum-assisted (or classical if expectations simulable) inversion via Buchberger + root finding, O(poly(n, log 1/eps)). Generic encodings (Sec IV B): black-box L-Lipschitz, grid search O(P(L/eps)^d) (Eq 47), "privacy breach is not possible" efficiently (Table I) -- this is where exponentially high frequency helps: perturbed GD needs O~(P L beta/eps^2) (Eq 48); frequency omega=O(exp(n)) makes it exponentially hard (sin(omega x) is omega-Lipschitz).
- Direct critique of Kumar 2309.13002 (their ref [28]) (Intro, Sec IV B.1, V, VI): (1) "overparameterization of a HEA leads to an untrainable model" (2-design, barren plateau) and needs exponential depth/parameters; (2) hardness stems from high frequencies, "may not be an exclusive quantum effect" (classical models can use such feature maps [33]); (3) "the privacy demonstrated in [28] was dependent on having an exponential DLA dimension. However, an exponentially large DLA also led to an untrainable model" (Sec IV B.1); (4) "having high frequency terms in the gradients ... does not necessarily protect against numerical snapshot inversion attacks" because gradients inherit the highest frequency of ALL snapshot entries while polynomially many low-frequency snapshots (e.g. first qubit of each Fourier-tower register has omega=1) may suffice for inversion; Alg 3 is "a completely classical snapshot inversion attack for the Fourier tower map" given a snapshot.
- Qualifier on their own attack ("attacks may not be practical for all VQCs"): slow Pauli expansion "unclear how strong of an assumption this is... privacy preservation may still be possible" (Sec III A). Conditions are SUFFICIENT for the attack, not necessary. They do not claim every VQC leaks; they say "we expect the majority of trainable VQC models to be vulnerable to weak privacy breaches" (Sec V A), i.e. LASA-type.
- Snapshot-recovery bound is tight in D: if D < dim(g), underdetermined; but that "would effectively lead to an underparameterized model" (Sec V A end).

### Threat model (Sec III)
Attacker receives: (1) all D gradients C_j = d y_theta(x)/d theta_j (gradient of MODEL OUTPUT, not of a cost; App B covers cost functions, e.g. MSE); (2) ansatz as ordered generators {theta_k, H_k} as polynomial combination of Pauli strings AND the parameters theta; (3) observable O satisfying LASA. Labels: since analysis is on y-gradients, labels/cost factors enter via App B (not read in detail -> UNVERIFIED for cost-function dependence). Strong breach additionally needs the encoding circuit (classically simulable expectation values, or quantum access to V(x') samples for quantum-assisted).

### Circuit / model
Generic: VQC y_theta(x)=Tr(U^dag(theta) O U(theta) rho(x)), n=Theta(d) qubits, U=prod exp(-i theta_k H_k) with D parameters (Eq 1-3). Not a specific circuit; encodings discussed: Pauli product (Eq 26), Fourier tower map (Eq 27, 50), general Pauli rotations, black-box. Numerics (Sec IV B.2, Figs 4-8) are only landscape illustrations of a single expectation value: R_X(x) sandwiched by arbitrary unitaries (Fig 4: frequency 1, independent of n) vs SU(2^n) gate U=exp(iHx) with random Hermitian H (Fig 5-7): spacing between stationary points r = 6.91 e^{-0.91 n} (Fig 7, n up to 6, avg over 10 H) -> frequency grows exponentially; Fig 8 two-input version n=1-3. No attack implementation, no success rates, no parameter counts.

### "Overparameterized" definition
In this paper the relevant quantity is dim(g) (dynamical Lie algebra, Def 1). Snapshot recovery needs D >= dim(g) (Thm 2) -- i.e. "overparameterized w.r.t. DLA" is exactly the regime where the linear system is determined and the weak breach is possible. If dim(g) = O(exp(n)): not recoverable by poly-time attacker but "likely untrainable" (Sec V A, citing barren-plateau results for LASA, Thm 6 in App C.4). Hence "a direct tradeoff between privacy and trainability."

### Noise
Not studied (grep: only mention is differential-privacy/artificial noise in intro). Thm 4 explicitly "ignore[s] potential errors in estimating these expectations"; quantum-assisted inversion would incur O(1/eps) via amplitude estimation (Sec IV A.2). Finite-shot snapshot estimation errors are therefore a stated gap (not analyzed).

### Stated limitations / future work (Sec V B, VI)
- Results are for LASA-type VQCs; beyond LASA privacy "becomes linked to ... whether there exist quantum variational models that are not classically simulatable and do not have barren plateaus" (Sec V A).
- Future privacy must come from the encoding: "feature maps that are untrainable with regard to adjusting x' to recover expectation values e_snap, while at the same time remaining useful" (Sec V B); open which datasets high-frequency feature maps can train on; noted "If the expectation values cannot be classically simulated then ... protected against any purely classical snapshot inversion" (necessary condition vs classical attacks).
- Slow Pauli expansion strength unclear; first-order inversion results do not give strict sufficient conditions "due to the possibility of unfavorable local minima".

## Cross-paper comparison (for a write-up)
- Kumar: ADAM + exact (param-shift) gradients, hardness measured by local-min count growing e^{1.48 m}; Papadopoulos: finite-difference + moving-average + Kalman works at 2 and 6 qubits but with angle encoding (1 freq per qubit), not m>=3 tower. Neither directly tests Kumar's n=1, m=3..6 regime with a smoothing attack -> an unclosed gap.
- Heredge: structural (algebraic) attack on snapshots, not an optimizer; requires D >= dim(g), LASA, slow Pauli expansion; also explains why Kumar's model resisted (exponential DLA = untrainable). Kumar's "overparameterized" (d >= 4Nq via [33]) vs Heredge's (D >= dim g) vs Papadopoulos's (equations >= unknowns) are three different notions.
- None of the three studies shot noise or hardware noise of the attacked circuit. Kumar asserts noise helps defender; Papadopoulos tests only Gaussian noise added to gradients.
